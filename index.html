QoLRC

About the center

The Quality of Life Research Center (QoLRC) studies how hematologic diseases and their treatment affect patients' everyday lives. We work with patient-reported outcomes (PROs) and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) across hematologic cancers and benign hematologic diseases.

The center was established in 2017 as a strategic investment by the Department of Hematology and the Hematology Research Unit at Odense University Hospital, and is affiliated with the University of Southern Denmark. QoLRC is the core facility for quality of life research in the Nordic Myeloma Study Group (NMSG), building on 35 years of experience with quality of life data from Nordic myeloma trials. We also work closely with the Nordic ITP Group on patient-reported outcomes in benign hematology.

Our work covers the whole path of patient-reported outcome research: planning and analyzing quality of life endpoints in clinical trials, following patients in routine care and population-based data, developing and validating questionnaires, and evaluating new models of care, such as home-based treatment and care adapted to older patients with frailty.

Our vision is to establish an interdisciplinary center for research in health-related quality of life at an international level, working closely with clinicians, statisticians, patient organizations and research partners across the Nordic countries and Europe.

Our research

  • PROs in clinical trials

    Designing, collecting and analyzing patient-reported outcomes in randomized and single-arm trials, following international standards for PRO endpoints and estimands.

  • Instrument development and validation

    Developing and psychometrically validating questionnaires so that what matters to patients can be measured reliably, using COSMIN-aligned methodology.

  • Multiple myeloma

    Quality of life during modern myeloma treatment, including measurable residual disease (MRD) guided strategies and long-term maintenance.

  • Autoimmune cytopenias

    Measuring the symptom burden of immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) and their treatment, including corticosteroids.

  • Older patients and frailty

    Using geriatric screening tools such as the G8 to understand how frailty relates to treatment tolerance and outcomes in older patients with cancer.

  • Patient involvement

    Working with patients and patient organizations so that our questions, methods and results reflect what matters most to the people we study.

  • October 28, 2026

    Research in Focus at the Department of Hematology

    Colleagues from across the Department of Hematology at Odense University Hospital gathered for the annual Research in Focus (Forskning i fokus) afternoon, organized by Henrik Frederiksen. The program covered acute leukemia and hematopathology, lymphoma and cellular therapies, multiple myeloma and amyloidosis, home-based treatment and supportive care, quality of life and population-based research, and benign hematology and frailty. Hans Henrik Knudsen, who is a patient representative in Lene Kongsgaard Nielsen's quality of life research at QoLRC, spoke about the role of patient involvement in research. From QoLRC, Henrik Frederiksen, Pia Pedersen, Tine Rosenberg, Anne Møller Clausen and Klara Rydahl presented their work.

  • October 19, 2026

    SSQ-MM validation study presented at ISOQOL 2026 in Prague

    Helena Møgelbjerg Ditzel presented a poster on the multinational psychometric validation of the Steroid Symptom Questionnaire – Multiple Myeloma (SSQ-MM) in patients with immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) and autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) at the 33rd Annual Conference of the International Society for Quality of Life Research (ISOQOL) in Prague. The study will include 180 patients in Denmark, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom, and will also develop a Swedish version of the questionnaire.

  • October 1, 2026

    Funding from Stiftelsen Dam for the REMNANT quality of life sub-study

    The patient-reported outcome sub-study of REMNANT has received funding from the Norwegian foundation Stiftelsen Dam, through the Norwegian Blood Cancer Society (Blodkreftforeningen). REMNANT is a Nordic randomized trial led by Oslo University Hospital, in which treatment of newly diagnosed multiple myeloma is guided by measurable residual disease. The grant supports the quality of life analyses in part 2 of the study, which are carried out at QoLRC and led by Helena Møgelbjerg Ditzel.
